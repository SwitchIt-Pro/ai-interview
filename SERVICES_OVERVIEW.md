# Conversational AI Interviewer - Services Overview

This document provides a simple, layman's explanation of what each component inside the `services/` directory does and how they work together to create a real-time, conversational AI interviewer. 

Our application is composed of several independent "services" that each perform one specific job for the AI.

## The Pipeline (How They Work Together)

1. The candidate speaks into the **Audio Service** (the microphone).
2. The **VAD Service** listens continuously to determine exactly when the speech starts and stops.
3. Once a complete sentence is recorded, it gets handed to the **STT Service**, which transcribes the audio into written text.
4. The text is fed into the **LLM Service** (the AI brain) to think of a professional reply.
5. As the AI thinks, it streams its text sentence-by-sentence to the **TTS Service**, which instantly turns the text back into a human-sounding voice and plays it through the speakers.
6. Meanwhile, the entire conversation is continuously being recorded in a text file by the **Transcript Service**.

---

## Service Breakdown

### 1. Audio Service (`audio_service.py`)
**Analogy: The Microphone & Waiting Line.**
This service handles direct communication with your computer's microphone. It keeps a queue of tiny audio chunks continuously coming in. It has a special function that acts as a gatekeeper: it drops chunks of purely silent audio but starts actively saving everything once someone actually begins speaking, compiling it all into one large recording until the person stops talking.

### 2. VAD Service (`vad_service.py`)
*Voice Activity Detection (Using Silero VAD)*
**Analogy: The Focused Listener.**
This is a specialized, lightweight AI model trained entirely to answer one question: *"Is a human currently talking in this audio chunk?"* It doesn't understand words, it just recognizes the sound of human speech. The pipeline uses this service to know exactly when to start recording the candidate and exactly when to cut off so the AI can start answering. It's smart enough to know that a 300ms noise (like a mic bump) isn't really a sentence.

### 3. STT Service (`stt_service.py`)
*Speech-to-Text (Using Whisper Small)*
**Analogy: The Court Reporter.**
This service takes the completed audio recording of the candidate and types it out verbatim into text. It uses an optimized Whisper model that is small enough to run on everyday hardware without freezing up the graphic card's memory. It also includes special AI "hallucination filters" to block out times when Whisper accidentally mistakes complete silence for phrases like *"Thank you!"* or *"Subtitles by..."*

### 4. LLM Service (`llm_service.py`)
*Large Language Model (Using Qwen2.5-1.5B)*
**Analogy: The AI Brain.**
This is where the actual interview logic happens. It remembers the system prompt (instructions to act like a polite, professional interviewer) and keeps a short-term memory of the conversation. When it receives the text from the candidate, it starts generating a reply. Crucially, it uses "streaming" generation, which means it spits out its answer word-by-word. This allows the system to start talking immediately without having to wait 5 seconds for the entire paragraph to be thought up.

### 5. TTS Service (`tts_service.py`)
*Text-to-Speech (Using Kokoro-82M)*
**Analogy: The Vocal Cords.**
This service takes the stream of words generated by the LLM and instantly synthesizes them into realistic, human-like voice audio. Because the LLM sends chunks continuously, the TTS can begin speaking sentence #1 while the LLM is still actively thinking up sentence #2, making the conversational latency feel completely seamless and natural. 

### 6. Transcript Service (`transcript_service.py`)
**Analogy: The Filing Cabinet.**
This component operates quietly in the background, keeping a pristine timestamped log of exactly who said what. Whenever the Candidate finishes speaking, or whenever the AI completes its thought, it writes an entry with a precise timestamp down onto a daily-generated text file in the `/logs` directory.
